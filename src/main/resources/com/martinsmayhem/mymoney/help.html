MyMoney Help

MyMoney {version} keeps your accounts, transactions, loans and statements in one encrypted file. This page covers the basics; hover over most fields and buttons for a tip about them.

Your MyMoney file

Everything is kept in one file (Name.MyMoney), encrypted with your password. A new file gets a folder of its own, named after it, and its attachments are kept in that folder too.

  • Open another file, or make a new one: File > Open (Ctrl+O). The file you open at startup is your default; MyMoney opens it straight away next time, asking for its password.
  • Back up: File > Back Up Database. See Backing up.
  • Rename: File > Rename File renames the file, and its folder with it.
  • Forget the password and the file can't be opened. There's no way around the encryption, so keep the password somewhere safe.

Backing up

File > Back Up Database makes an encrypted copy of your database (your accounts and transactions), named for the file and the time: Household_20261016_0845.MyMoney. It opens with the same password. Backups go in the folder set in Edit > Preferences > Backups; the first time, MyMoney asks for one and remembers it. Choose a folder on another drive, so a backup survives this one failing.

Attachments and statements aren't in a database backup. They're in the attachments folder beside your MyMoney file. To back up everything, back up that whole folder with a backup tool, such as FreeFileSync (free, for Linux, Windows and macOS: freefilesync.org), or your system's own: Déjà Dup on Linux, File History on Windows, Time Machine on macOS.

Also in Preferences > Backups:

  • A reminder: when you close MyMoney after using a file a number of times since its last backup, it offers to back it up (0: never ask).
  • Old backups: keep only so many backups a day (the latest), and after so many days, only the last backup of each day. Only this file's own backups in the backup folder are ever removed. Both are off (0) to start with, keeping every backup.

To use a backup, open it with File > Open, or copy it over your MyMoney file (with MyMoney closed) and rename it to match.

Accounts

The tree on the left lists your accounts by type (checking, savings, credit cards, loans, cash, other assets, envelopes), with their balances if you turn that on in Edit > Preferences. Summary at the top shows your net worth and every account at a glance.

  • Add, change or remove accounts: Account > New Account, Edit Account, Manage Accounts. Manage Accounts holds each account's details: bank, account and routing numbers, website, and for loans and cards, their terms.
  • An account can go under another (a sub-account), like savings envelopes under a savings account.
  • Right-click an account in the tree for its most-used actions.

The ledger: entering transactions

Pick an account to see its ledger. The blank row (grey) is for a new transaction: type into it, and it's saved as soon as it has an amount. Ctrl+N jumps to it.

  • Dates are typed as {dateFormat}, or as 8 digits with nothing between, or without the year (this year). F4 or Alt+Down opens a calendar. The format is set in Edit > Preferences.
  • Amounts: money out goes in Payment, money in in Deposit. You can type sums, like 45.10+12.99. With insert the decimal point on in Preferences, 1234 means $12.34.
  • Payee, Category, Tag and Type suggest as you type; Enter or Space picks the highlighted one. A payee you've used before brings its last category along.
  • Status (the C column): Cleared once your bank shows it, Reconciled once it's on a reconciled statement; also Pending, Paid and Scheduled. Reconciled transactions are locked against most changes.
  • Enter saves and moves down to the next transaction (or, if you choose so in Preferences, to the next field, like Tab).
  • Several at once: select rows, then right-click to change their date, payee, category, tag or status together, or delete them.
  • In the wrong account? Right-click > Move to Account moves the selected transactions to another account. A transfer stays linked with its other account, and attachments move too. Reconciled transactions aren't moved.
  • Columns: right-click a column heading to choose which are shown; drag a heading to move it; double-click the line between headings to fit a column to its contents; click a heading to sort (Ctrl+click to sort by a second column too).
  • Future transactions are listed last, in grey, and don't count toward today's balance.

Categories and transfers

  • A category says what money was for: Food:Groceries is Groceries under Food. Type a new one and it's made for you.
  • A transfer moves money between your accounts: put the other account in brackets as the category, like [Savings]. The matching transaction appears in the other account by itself, and the two stay in step.
  • Paying a loan or a card from checking is a transfer to it, like [Car Loan].

Finding transactions

Type in the Filter box above the ledger (Ctrl+F) to show only transactions with that text in any column; Escape clears it. Balances still count everything.

  • Start with a column's name and a colon to look in just that column: payee: walmart, memo: card 2549, type:pos, payment: 69.00.
  • Hide reconciled hides reconciled transactions.
  • The bar at the bottom totals whatever transactions you select.

Importing from your bank

File > Import reads a file downloaded from your bank's website:

  • QFX or OFX (Quicken or Money format) and QIF files of one account's transactions. Each download is shown to you one at a time: match it to a transaction you'd already entered, or add it as new. Matches are suggested for you.
  • Which account: a QFX/OFX file says which account it's for, so the account with that account number is chosen. If none has it (say, in a new file with no accounts yet), a new account is made for it, named for the bank, the account type and the number's last four digits, like WF Checking 2222. Rename it any time in Manage Accounts.
  • Afterwards, the downloaded file can be left where it is, deleted, or moved into MyMoney's folder as Account/imports/YYYY-MM-DD.qfx. QFX and OFX files hold your account number in plain text, so MyMoney checks before moving one there.
  • A QIF from Quicken with every account brings in the whole file: accounts, categories and transactions.
  • When you change a downloaded payee's name ("WALMART #1234" to "Walmart"), MyMoney remembers it for next time (Account > Manage Payee Renames).
  • The bank's transaction type (POS, DIRECTDEBIT, ...) goes in the Type column.

Scheduled transactions

Account > Scheduled Transactions lists bills and paychecks that repeat. MyMoney puts each one in the ledger ahead of time (14 days, or as set in Preferences), marked Scheduled, so you can see what's coming. Right-click a transaction and choose Add to Schedule to make one from it.

Reconciling

Reconciling checks an account against its statement, so you know every transaction is right. Account > Reconcile Account (Ctrl+R):

  1. Enter the statement's ending balance and date. Transactions already Cleared, from your last reconciliation through that date, are selected for you. Tick As of to select every unreconciled one in that period.
  2. Click the C box (or press Space) on each transaction that's on the statement, and off any that isn't. Edit, add or delete transactions right there if you need to.
  3. When the Difference is $0.00, click Done: they're all Reconciled. Attach the statement file under Bank Statement while you're there.

Not finished? Finish Later keeps your place. If the difference won't come to zero, check the ending balance you typed against the statement first.

Statements and attachments

  • Statements: Account > Statements keeps any account's statements: add, open, rename, re-date or remove them. Statements attached while reconciling are listed there too.
  • Attachments: receipts and other files for a transaction. Click its paperclip column (or right-click > Attachments) to add or open them.
  • Files are copied into the attachments folder beside your MyMoney file, filed and named for you:
    attachments/statements/Account/YYYY/Account_statement_YYYY-MM-DD
    attachments/transactions/Account/YYYY/MM/DD/id-payee
    The file you added stays where it was. Changing a transaction's date or payee renames its files to match.
  • Adding and removing files can't be undone (they're files on your disk).

Loans and credit cards

Enter a loan's terms in Manage Accounts (rate, original amount, term, origination and first payment dates, and how the lender charges interest), and MyMoney works out the interest in every payment.

  • Interest Charged: Monthly (rate ÷ 12; many mortgages), Daily, from the day paid (most auto and personal loans), or Daily, on the due dates (Affirm and other pay-over-time loans).
  • A loan's balance is what's left to pay, interest included, as lenders show it. The top panel also shows the principal remaining, and the total interest and amount paid.
  • In a loan's ledger, Payment is money paid toward it and Charge is more owed. The Interest and Principal columns are worked out for you; type over either for an interest-only or principal-only payment, or to match the lender's figures.
  • Account > Amortization Schedule shows every payment of a loan (and how much sooner extra principal would pay it off); Payoff Schedule does the same for a card; Debt Snowball plans paying off several debts, smallest first.

Undo

Edit > Undo (Ctrl+Z) reverses your last change: an edit, a new or deleted transaction, an import, a reconciliation. Edit > Redo (Ctrl+Y) puts it back. Undo starts afresh each time a file is opened.

Where things are kept

  • Your data: your MyMoney file, its attachments folder, and any imported bank files (Account/imports), all in the MyMoney file's folder, wherever you made it. Back up that whole folder (see Backing up).
  • Database backups: in the backup folder you chose.
  • Settings (recent files, preferences): in your settings folder, shown in Help > About. They hold no financial data.
  • Installing, updating or removing MyMoney never changes either.
